## Central claims

- The phase space of a light quantum with respect to a given volume is expressed in "cells" of the size $h^3$ divided.
- The number of possible distributions of the light quanta of a macroscopically defined radiation among these cells provides the entropy and thus all the thermodynamic properties of the radiation.
- Planck's formula for the distribution of energy in the radiation of the black body forms the starting point for quantum theory, which has been developed over the last 20 years and has borne rich fruit in all fields of physics.
- He recognized the logical deficiency of all previous derivatives and tried to deduce the formula independently of classical theory.
- However, in order to bring this formula into harmony with Planck's, he must make use of Vienna's law of displacement and Bohr's principle of correspondence.
- On the other hand, the light quantum hypothesis in connection with statistical mechanics (as it has been adapted by Planck to the needs of quantum theory) seems to me to be sufficient for the derivation of the law independently of classical theory.
- If we divide the entire phase volume into cells of the size h³, the frequency range d ν thus belongs to 4π V $$\frac{\nu^{2}}{c^{2}}$$ d ν cells.
- However, the total number of cells must be regarded as the number of possible arrangements of a quantum in the given volume.
- Be $p 0^s$ the number of vacant cells, $p 1^s$ the number of those that contain a quantum, $p 2^s$ the number of cells containing two quanta, etc.
- The method used here also provides the quantum theory of the ideal gas, as I will explain elsewhere.
