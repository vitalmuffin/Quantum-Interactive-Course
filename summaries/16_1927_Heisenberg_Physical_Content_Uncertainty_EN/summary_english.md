### 1.3 THE PHYSICAL CONTENT OF QUANTUM KINEMATICS AND MECHANICS

- Starting from the basic principles thus obtained, we show how microscopic processes can be understood by way of quantum mechanics (.3).
- We believe we understand the physical content of a theory when we can see its qualitative experimental consequences in all simple cases and when at the same time we have checked that the application of the theory never contains inner contradictions.
- Already from this circumstance, one might conclude that no interpretation of quantum mechanics is possible, which uses ordinary kinematical and mechanical concepts.
- The question therefore arises whether, through a more precise analysis of these kinematic and mechanical concepts, it might be possible to clear up the contradictions evident up to now in the physical interpretations of quantum mechanics and to arrive at a physical understanding of the quantum-mechanical formulas.

### §4. DISCUSSION OF A FEW SPECIAL IDEALIZED EXPERIMENTS — Argument block 1

- This atom is sent through an inhomogeneous magnetic field for the determination of its energy level, as is usual in the Stern-Gerlach experiment; yet we also have the inhomogeneous field follow the atomic beam over a long stretch of its path.
- The phase relation between atom and external radiation may be determined, for example, by the phase relations of large numbers of atoms with one another ([R. W.] Wood's experiments).
- In a well-known idealized experiment proposed by Bohr, the atoms of a Stern-Gerlach atomic beam are first excited to a resonance fluorescence at a definite state by incident radiation.
- After the atoms have passed the magnetic field, however, there can hardly be any doubt that the atomic beam has divided into two beams of which one corresponds to atoms in the upper state, the other in the lower.
- Therefore, it is meaningless to speak of the "energy of the atom." Also, after the atom has left the radiation field one is not entitled to say that it is in a definite stationary state, insofar as one enquires about the coherence properties of the radiation.
