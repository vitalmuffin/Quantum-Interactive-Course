## On the connection between the closure of the electron groups in the atom and the complex structure of the spectra.

By W. Pauli jr. in Hamburg.

(Received January 16, 1925.)

It is proposed, especially with regard to the Millikan-Landé finding of the representability of the alkali doublets by relativistic formulas and on the basis of results obtained in an earlier work, that these doublets and their anomalous Zeeman effect express a classically indescribable ambiguity of the quantum theoretical properties of the luminous electron, without the completed noble gas configuration of the atomic residue in the form of of a trunk impulse or as the seat of the magneto-mechanical anomaly of the atom. Then an attempt is made to pursue this point of view, which has been adopted as a provisional working hypothesis, as far as possible in its consequences, despite the fundamental difficulties that oppose it, even with regard to atoms other than alkalis. First of all, it is shown that, contrary to the usual view, in the case of a strong external magnetic field, where the coupling forces between the atomic residue and the luminous electron can be dispensed with, it makes it possible to ascribe to these two subsystems no other properties than the free atomic residue or the luminous electron in the alkalis with regard to the number of their stationary states and the values of their quantum numbers and their magnetic energy. On the basis of this result, a general classification of each electron in the atom by the principal quantum number is arrived at $n$ and two minor quantum numbers $k_1$ and $k_2$, to which, in the presence of an external field, another quantum number $m_1$ is added. Following on from a recent work by E. C. Stoner, this classification leads to a general quantum-theoretical formulation of the closure of the electron groups in the atom.

Before we apply this quantum-theoretical classification of electrons in the atom to the problem of the closure of electron groups in the following paragraph, we must discuss in more detail the difficulties that stand in the way of the view of complex structure and anomalous Zeeman effects proposed here, and the limitation of their importance.

First of all, this view does not do direct justice to the in many respects independent, separate occurrence of the various term systems (e.g., in the case of alkaline earths, that of the single and triplet systems), which also applies in the position of the terms of these systems and in Landé's interval rule. Certainly, one cannot assume two different causes for the energy differences of the triplet levels in the alkaline earths, both the anomaly of the relativity correction of the luminous electron and the dependence of the interaction energy of the electron with the atomic residue on the orientation of these two systems to each other.

In general, it should be noted that the considerations given here concerning the transition from a strong to a weak or vanishing field are in principle based on the invariance of the statistical weights of the quantum states. However, there do not seem to be any indications of the existence of a connection between the problem of the closure of the electron groups in the atom and the correspondence principle, as suspected by Bohr, on the basis of the results obtained. The problem of the more detailed justification of the general rule on the occurrence of equivalent electrons in the atom on which this is based is likely to be successfully attacked only after a further deepening of the basic principles of quantum theory.
