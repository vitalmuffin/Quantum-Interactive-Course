## On quantum mechanics. II.

By M. Born, W. Heisenberg and P. Jordan in Göttingen.

(Received November 16, 1925.)

The quantum mechanics developed from Heisenberg's approaches in Part I of this thesis is extended to systems of arbitrary degrees of freedom. The perturbation theory is carried out for non-degenerate and a large class of degenerate systems and its connection with the eigenvalue theory of Hermitian forms is demonstrated. The results obtained are used to derive the theorems about momentum and angular momentum and to derive selection rules and intensity formulas. Finally, the approaches of the theory are applied to the statistics of the natural oscillations of a cavity.

Introduction. The present work attempts to further develop the theory of general quantum theoretical mechanics, the physical and mathematical foundations of which are presented in two previous works by the authors¹⁾. It proved possible to extend the theory to systems of several degrees of freedom²⁾ (ch. 2) and, by introducing the "canonical transformations", to reduce the problem of integrating the equations of motion to known mathematical problems; on the one hand, this theory of canonical transformations resulted in a theory of perturbation (chap. 1, § 4), which shows a far-reaching similarity to the classical perturbation theory, and on the other hand, a connection between quantum mechanics and the mathematically highly developed theory of the quadratic forms of infinitely many variables (chap. 3). But before we enter into the presentation of this further development of the theory, we shall try to define its physical content more precisely.

The starting point of the theory attempted was the conviction that it would not be possible to master the difficulties which we have encountered at every turn in quantum theory, especially in recent years, until a mathematical system of relations between principally observable quantities of similar magnitudes is available for the mechanics of atomic and electron motions.
