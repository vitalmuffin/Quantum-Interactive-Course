One further general remark should be made here. Almost all theories of thermal radiation are based on an examination of the interactions between radiation and molecules. Generally, however, one confines oneself to considering the exchange of energy, without taking the exchange of momentum into account. One is easily tempted to do so, because the smallness of the momentum transferred by radiation means that, in reality, the latter almost always takes a back seat to other causes of motion. However, for theoretical consideration, these small effects must be regarded as entirely equivalent to the more obvious effects of energy transfer by radiation, since energy and momentum are most closely linked; a theory can therefore only be regarded as valid once it has been shown that the momentum transferred from radiation to matter, as described by that theory, leads to the kinds of motion required by the theory of heat.

## On the darkening curves of channel rays on dry plates.

By M. Wolfke.

(With Table I.)

As is well known, there are two distinct photographic effects caused by canal rays. With short exposures, blackening occurs at the points struck by canal rays; with longer exposures, a form of solarisation¹ occurs.

With a view to applying channel rays to a number of new fields of research, I set myself the task, above all, of obtaining sharp parabolas using the shortest possible exposure times. The solarisation method is not suitable for this purpose, as it requires a longer exposure time; consequently, I had to rely solely on the blackening method.
