## Interaction of neutral atoms and homeopolar bonding according to quantum mechanics$^{1}$.

By W. Heitler and F. London in Zurich.

With 2 illustrations. (Received June 30, 1927.)

The interplay of forces between neutral atoms shows a characteristic quantum mechanical ambiguity. This ambiguity seems to be suitable to encompass the various behaviors that experience provides: in the case of hydrogen, for example, the possibility of a homeopolar bond, or elastic reflection, but in the case of noble gases only the latter — and this already as effects of the first approximation of approximately the right size. In the selection and discussion of the various behaviors, the Pauli principle also proves itself here, in application to systems of several atoms.

The interaction between neutral atoms has so far caused considerable difficulties for theoretical treatment. While it had been possible to get a simple picture of the attractive forces of ions for a long time, the conditions for neutral atoms, especially the possibility of a non-polar bond, seemed extremely difficult to understand if one did not want to resort to very artificial explanations$^{2}$.

The development of quantum mechanics has certainly provided new points of view for the treatment of these problems: First, the charge distribution in the new "models" is completely different$^{3}$ than in Bohr's models (i.e. decay as $e^{-r}$), which would already entail a completely different interplay of forces between "neutral" atoms. However, more essential and decisive for the understanding of the possible behaviors between neutral atoms is a characteristic quantum mechanical beating phenomenon, which is closely related to the resonance beats found by Heisenberg. We will study these relationships using the example of two H atoms (§ 1) and two He atoms (§ 3). To anticipate the result (§ 2): One obtains two solutions for the interaction energy: One which causes attraction at medium distances of atoms, and at small distances between atoms.
